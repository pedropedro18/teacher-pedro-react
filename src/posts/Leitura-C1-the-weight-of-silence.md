There is a particular kind of silence that settles over a house after an argument — not the absence of sound, but the presence of everything left unsaid. Beatriz had grown accustomed to it over the years, the way one grows accustomed to a persistent ache: not by ignoring it, but by learning to move around it.

Her husband, João, sat across the kitchen table, his coffee long gone cold, his eyes fixed on some indeterminate point beyond the window. They had disagreed, again, about something that had seemed trivial at first — whether to sell the old family house in the countryside — but which had, as such conversations often do, unravelled into a broader reckoning with everything neither of them had voiced in years.

"It isn't really about the house," Beatriz finally said, breaking the silence that had stretched between them like a taut wire. "You know that, don't you?"

João didn't answer immediately. He turned his cup slowly on its saucer, a habit she recognised as his way of buying time, of composing thoughts too unwieldy to articulate spontaneously.

"I know," he admitted at last. "I suppose I've been holding onto that house because it's easier than admitting what I'm actually afraid of losing."

Beatriz felt something loosen in her chest — not resolution, not yet, but the faint possibility of it. Conversations like this one rarely resolved anything outright; they merely opened a door, however narrow, through which understanding might eventually pass.

Outside, the late afternoon light shifted, casting long shadows across the kitchen floor, and for a moment, neither of them spoke. But it was, Beatriz thought, a different kind of silence altogether.

## Vocabulary Notes

- *to settle over* — instalar-se sobre, pairar sobre
- *taut* — esticado, tenso
- *unwieldy* — difícil de manejar, complicado
- *reckoning* — acerto de contas, confronto (com a realidade)
- *to unravel* — desenrolar-se, desmoronar-se
