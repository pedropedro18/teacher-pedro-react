Maria arrived in Lisbon on a cold October morning. She had never travelled alone before, and her hands were shaking slightly as she pulled her suitcase through the narrow cobblestone streets. The city was quiet, except for the distant sound of a tram bell ringing somewhere near the river.

She had booked a small room above a café called O Cantinho. The owner, an old man named Sr. António, welcomed her with a warm smile and a cup of coffee she hadn't even asked for.

"You look tired," he said in careful English. "Sit down. The room can wait."

Maria sat by the window, watching the street outside. People walked quickly, wrapped in scarves, carrying bags of fresh bread. A woman was selling flowers on the corner, singing softly to herself.

"Is this your first time in Portugal?" Sr. António asked, placing a plate of pastéis de nata on the table.

"Yes," Maria replied. "I came here to write. I used to work in an office, but I always wanted to write a book."

Sr. António nodded slowly, as if he understood something she hadn't said out loud.

"Many people come to Lisbon looking for something," he said. "The city is patient. It waits for you to find it."

Maria smiled for the first time since she had left home. She didn't know yet what she was looking for, but somehow, sitting in that small café, she felt she was closer to finding it.

Outside, the tram bell rang again, and the city slowly woke up.
